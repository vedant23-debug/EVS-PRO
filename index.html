04 / THE HUMAN FACTOR

Small habits can create
big risks.

Water safety does not stop at treatment. Containers, hands, taps and filling areas can all become part of the journey.

01
🧴

Dirty bottles

Reusable bottles need regular cleaning. Residues and moisture can support microbial growth.

→ CLEAN REGULARLY
02
🖐️

Dirty hands

Touching bottle openings, taps or dispensing points with unclean hands can introduce contamination.

→ WASH HANDS
03
🚰

Unclean filling areas

Dirty surfaces, standing water and damaged fixtures can turn a clean source into a hygiene concern.

→ KEEP AREAS CLEAN
04
🔄

Sharing bottles

Sharing personal drinking containers can increase the opportunity for transfer of microbes between people.

→ USE YOUR OWN

06 / THE JOURNEY

How contamination
travels.

Follow one fictional droplet from source to bottle — then see how each stage can interrupt the chain.

01Source

Contamination can enter a water source through sewage, runoff or other pathways.

02Treatment

Water treatment can reduce or remove many contaminants when properly designed and operated.

03Distribution

Infrastructure, storage and interruptions can influence water quality before the tap.

04Tap

Dirty fixtures and surrounding surfaces can become a hygiene concern.

05Bottle

A poorly cleaned container can undo good habits earlier in the chain.

✓
THE INTERRUPTION

Safety is a chain — protect every link.

Source protection + appropriate treatment + clean infrastructure + clean containers + good personal hygiene = a stronger safety chain.

07 / WHY IT MATTERS

Unsafe water can
carry disease.

Contaminated drinking water can transmit several infectious diseases. The examples below are educational, not a diagnostic tool.

01

Diarrhoeal disease

Unsafe water and sanitation can contribute to diarrhoeal illness, especially when microbial contamination is present.

02

Cholera

Cholera is strongly associated with contaminated water and inadequate sanitation and hygiene.

03

Typhoid

Typhoid fever can spread through food or water contaminated with the bacteria that cause the disease.

04

Hepatitis A

Hepatitis A can spread through ingestion of contaminated food or water.

05

Dysentery

Some forms of dysentery can be linked to ingestion of water contaminated with infectious organisms.

06

Polio

Poliovirus can spread through the faecal–oral route, including through contaminated water in settings with poor sanitation.
